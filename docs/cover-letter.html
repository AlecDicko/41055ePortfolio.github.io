Selection-Criteria Cover Letter

Dear Mr/Mrs Industrus,

I am writing to apply for a graduate engineering position in Industrus Engineering's Graduate Program. I am a final-semester mechatronic engineering student at the University of Technology Sydney who works comfortably across the full stack of a system, from analogue signal conditioning through firmware to the mechanical hardware it drives, and that range fits well with a program built around rotating graduates across disciplines and project types.

3.1 A commitment to ethical conduct and the highest standards of professional accountability

While setting up programmable logic controllers (PLCs) for a teaching initiative I am putting together ahead of it being formally added into the subject, I acquired a pair of DIN mounted power supplies that would have required me to wire directly into mains power. I needed to power the units without breaching New South Wales regulations governing unlicensed electrical work, even though I was confident I had the technical skill to complete the wiring myself. Rather than proceeding on the strength of that confidence alone, I held the work back and sought proper guidance on what I was and was not authorised to do. The result was a fully compliant setup where I never handled mains wiring myself, and the initiative stayed on track without putting me, the lab or the equipment at risk.

3.2 Demonstrated ability to effectively communicate both with other engineers and with stakeholders from different fields

As part of my internship, I regularly put engineering suggestions to my supervisor, including a proposal to split the printed circuit boards on a project for BTP to better use the space inside the hub control module and its chassis. That particular suggestion was not taken forward, but it is typical of an ongoing exchange of recommendations between us. In another instance, I approached a lab manager to check what electrical work was permitted within the lab space, and he advised that an electrician would be required to complete my original plan. Using his guidance that IEC 60320 connectors, a standardised connector for appliance power leads, were allowable if pre-wired, I redesigned the system to work only with the low voltage DC side, avoiding the mains AC entirely and keeping the project moving without needing an external electrician.

3.3 The ability to engage with a creative, innovative and proactive environment

While watching how our Dobot Magician robots, small desktop robotic arms used for demonstrations, had been set up for previous tour groups, I noticed the camera mounts being used were not properly engineered for the job. I took the initiative to design and 3D print a better one. I built a mount with just enough clearance to keep the robot's full range of movement unobstructed, while keeping the design solid enough to hold up and simple enough to print without support material. The mounts were rolled out generally into the kits the robots are stored with, and while direct feedback was limited, seeing them still in use by the next group through further demonstrations was enough to prove they were successful.

3.4 Demonstrated ability to use and manage information

I was brought onto the FX3U project, a Mitsubishi PLC model, with only a forum post, a link to a GitHub repository, and a preference from my supervisor for OpenPLC, an open-source PLC programming platform, over Mitsubishi's own MELSOFT software. I needed to turn that into a working, repeatable pipeline that other students without much coding experience could actually use. I resolved a build error that was stopping OpenPLC Editor from launching, then tracked down and fixed an architecture mismatch that was stopping the uploader from working on a Raspberry Pi, extending the pipeline beyond my own laptop. Since the subject this supports isn't built around writing code, I also wrote a document defining exact rules for cleaning up the raw program exports so they upload correctly every time, which now guides how AI tools are used to help students edit their code without needing to debug the export by hand.

3.5 The ability to manage your own performance in a professional environment

Between casual work, paid tutoring and my internship, I was juggling three sets of hours every week, and tracking them by hand after the fact was leading to mistakes. I needed a way to record my time accurately enough that my pay across all three roles was always correct. I built a timesheet system in Excel with Visual Basic macros, covering pay rates for each task, category groupings, weekend multipliers and paid break checkboxes, and switched from logging hours after the fact to logging them as I worked, and had the embedded macros security reviewed before relying on it. The result was a timesheet that stayed accurate week to week, with a clear structure that made sure my reported hours across all three roles were always correct.

3.6 A demonstrated ability to work as part of a team and to show leadership when required

Students working on the BTP project for their capstones were struggling to keep their wiring consistent and correct, not because they lacked ability but because they lacked experience. I taught them proper soldering and crimping technique, supplying a number of connectors from home so they had enough parts to practise on. That gave them the time and hands on experience to improve. The relevant phase of the project was ultimately completed, and it will continue into the future.

I am excited to continue this conversation and look forward to hearing from you.

Regards,

Alec
